Thanksgiving Dinner on a Budget in 2026: What Groceries Cost and Where to Save

Eggs and butter cost less than last November, the turkey is cheap in the ads, and a few things are up. Here's how to plan the meal around your paychecks.

By Chris HardawayNovember 17, 20267 min readEducation, not advice

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. If you're hosting, the meal is one of the bigger single grocery runs of the year, and it lands in the same stretch as Black Friday, early holiday shopping and the first cold-weather utility bills. The good news this year: some of the classic Thanksgiving staples cost less than they did last November, and the turkey itself is cheap if you shop the ads. The catch: a few other things are up, and the cheapest birds often come with strings attached.

Here's what the official numbers say about grocery prices right now, a simple way to set a Thanksgiving budget, and where the real savings are.

What grocery prices are doing in November 2026

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 10 that grocery prices (what it calls "food at home") rose 0.4 percent in October, one month on its own. Inside that, dairy rose 0.6 percent, fruits and vegetables 0.6 percent, and meats, poultry, fish and eggs 0.5 percent. Cereals and bakery products were unchanged, and nonalcoholic beverages fell 0.3 percent.

One caveat before you trust any "prices are up X percent from last year" headline this season: BLS never collected October 2025 prices because of the lapse in federal funding, so it couldn't publish its usual 12-month changes for October 2026. To compare with last Thanksgiving, the cleanest option is BLS's own average-price data, matching October 2026 with November 2025, the month of last year's dinner.

So the baking side of the meal (eggs, butter, flour, sugar) is a little cheaper than last year, and the cheese board and the bread basket are a little pricier. For the rest of the year, the U.S. Department of Agriculture's latest Food Price Outlook (October 23) expects grocery prices for all of 2026 to come in about 2.3 percent above 2025, with eggs down sharply, fresh vegetables up about 5.8 percent, sugar and sweets up about 6.5 percent, and beef up about 8.7 percent.

The turkey: cheap in the ads, with conditions

BLS doesn't publish a current national average price for whole turkey, but the USDA tracks what grocery chains advertise. Its weekly report dated November 13 found a weighted average advertised price of 96 cents a pound for a conventional frozen whole turkey under 16 pounds, down from $1.19 the week before and about the same as the 98 cents a year earlier. Fresh conventional birds averaged $1.90 a pound in the same report.

The same USDA report adds a detail worth knowing: some of the lowest-priced frozen birds require an additional purchase, typically $20 to $50.

A turkey "deal" that needs a $40 minimum purchase is only a deal if the $40 is food already on your list.

A round, made-up example shows why. A 14-pound frozen turkey at 96 cents a pound costs $13.44. The same bird at $1.19 a pound, with no strings, costs $16.66. The difference is $3.22. If hitting the minimum makes you add $15 of things you wouldn't have bought, the "cheap" turkey cost you more. If your list already runs over $40, take the deal.

And fresh versus frozen: the same 14 pounds at the $1.90 fresh average is $26.60, $13.16 more than the frozen ad price. Frozen works well if you have fridge space and buy early enough to thaw it safely in the refrigerator, which takes days, not hours.

How to set a Thanksgiving budget in four steps

  1. Write the menu and the head count. The menu drives the cost more than prices do. Ten people and six sides is a different meal from ten people and three.
  2. Price it from your store's ad and your last receipt. Check what's in the pantry first: the half bag of flour and the spices from last year are money you've already spent.
  3. Pad the lines that are rising. Based on the numbers above, give cheese, bread, fresh vegetables and anything sweet a little extra room. Eggs and butter can probably stay near last year's amounts.
  4. Pick a total and a per-guest number. A made-up example: a $160 grocery list for 10 people is $16 a person. If four guests each bring a dish worth about $15, the host's list drops to about $100.

If you're bringing a dish instead of hosting, the same steps work in miniature: price the one recipe, then decide whether it goes on this week's grocery trip or the next one.

Split the shopping across your paychecks

A Thanksgiving budget can be the right size and still squeeze your checking account, because it all tends to happen in one week, often a few days before payday. Splitting the shopping into two trips helps:

  • Trip one, after this week's payday: the frozen turkey (if the ad price is good), flour, sugar, canned goods, broth, stuffing mix, foil pans, anything that keeps.
  • Trip two, the weekend before: produce, dairy, bread, fresh herbs, and anything you ran short on.

Then look at the week of the 26th as a whole. Thanksgiving groceries, Black Friday, travel gas and any bills due that week all come out of the same balance. Walk it forward day by day and find the lowest point. If it gets close to zero, move a purchase to after the next payday. Where the real savings are

  • Ask for dishes early and specifically. "Bring a side" gets you three bags of rolls. "Could you bring the green beans for ten?" gets you green beans.
  • Lean on the cheaper staples this year. Pies, rolls from scratch and egg-heavy sides cost a little less than last year. A cheese board costs more.
  • Buy the bird by need, not by size. Count guests and decide how many leftovers you actually want before you pick a size. A bigger bird than you need is money you eat as leftovers for a week.
  • Use store brands for the basics. Broth, canned pumpkin, cranberry sauce, butter and stuffing mix are where store brands are hardest to tell apart.
  • Plan the leftovers. Soup, sandwiches and a casserole on Friday and Saturday turn a big grocery bill into three or four meals instead of one.
  • Watch the card balance. If the groceries go on a credit card, pay them off with the next statement. At the Fed's latest average rate for cards that charge interest, a balance carried into the new year adds up quickly; see what a December rate hike would mean for your card.

If money is tight enough that the meal itself is the problem, it's okay to scale down or to ask for help. Local food banks and churches often run Thanksgiving programs, and dialing 211 connects you with food help near you.

Three things to do this week

  1. Write the menu and head count, then ask guests for specific dishes.
  2. Price the list from your store's ad, padding cheese, bread, vegetables and sweets.
  3. Split the shopping into two trips around your paydays, and check the week of the 26th for its lowest balance.

The bottom line

Thanksgiving 2026 doesn't have to be expensive. Baking staples and eggs cost less than last year, the turkey is cheap in the ads if the conditions fit your list, and cheese, bread and vegetables need a little extra room. Write the menu, share the dishes, split the shopping across your paychecks, and check that the week of the 26th never runs your balance too low.

Questions people ask

How much does Thanksgiving dinner cost in 2026?

It depends on the menu and the head count. As a made-up example, a $160 grocery list for 10 people works out to $16 a person. Official data show eggs, butter and flour cost a little less than last November, while cheese, bread and milk cost a little more.

How much is a turkey per pound this year?

In the USDA's weekly grocery ad report dated November 13, 2026, conventional frozen whole turkeys under 16 pounds averaged 96 cents a pound in ads, and fresh conventional birds averaged $1.90. Some of the lowest prices required an additional purchase of $20 to $50.

Are grocery prices going up for Thanksgiving?

BLS reported grocery prices rose 0.4 percent in October 2026. The USDA expects 2026 grocery prices to average about 2.3 percent above 2025, with eggs much lower and vegetables, sweets and beef higher.

What is the cheapest way to host Thanksgiving?

Write the menu first, ask guests for specific dishes, buy the turkey by need rather than size, use store brands for staples, plan the leftovers, and split the shopping across two paychecks.
